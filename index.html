How it works

From key press to light, in four steps.

No cloud, no account. Your piano, a small computer and a strip of lights talk to each other in your room.

  1. 01

    You play the piano

    It sends every note over a USB cable (that's MIDI) to the Raspberry Pi.

  2. 02

    The Pi listens

    A Raspberry Pi running LEDsplay hears each note in real time and decides what to light.

  3. 03

    The keys light up

    An LED strip along the keyboard glows above the key you pressed, or the next key in a song.

  4. 04

    You choose on your phone

    Open ledsplay.local in any browser to pick songs, modes and colours.

FAQ

Short answers.

More detail in the install guide.

Which digital pianos work with LEDsplay?

Any digital piano with a USB port for MIDI. Look on the back for a port labelled “USB TO HOST” or “USB COMPUTER”; it's usually the square kind (USB-B). A port for USB sticks won't work. LEDsplay is tested on a Roland FP-30.

How much does it cost in total?

About $60 for all the parts with a Raspberry Pi Zero 2 WH. The software is free to start; Pro is $9 once, if you want it.

Which Raspberry Pi do I need?

A Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W, 3B, 3B+ or 4B. Get the Zero 2 WH: its pins come already soldered, while the plain Zero 2 W needs them soldered on. The Zero also fits the optional PCB and case. A Pi 5 isn't supported.

Do I need to solder?

No, it's optional. Buy an LED strip sold as 2 m in a single piece and plug the signal wires onto the Pi's pins. The strip's two power wires can be twisted onto the supply cable and covered with heat-shrink, or soldered if you prefer. The hardware list covers the details.

How many LEDs does an 88-key piano need?

177 LEDs, which is about 1.23 m of a WS2812B strip with 144 LEDs per metre. Buy it as one 2 m piece and cut it to length. The hardware list has the details.

Is it hard to set up?

You don't need a terminal. Write the LEDsplay image to a microSD card with Raspberry Pi Imager and start the Pi. On your phone, join the LEDSplay-Setup Wi‑Fi and pick your home network, then open ledsplay.local in your browser. Prefer the command line? A one-line install script takes about 5 minutes.
