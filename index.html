How it works

From key press to light, in four steps.

Nothing goes to the internet. Your piano, a small computer and a strip of lights talk to each other in your room.

  1. 01

    You play the piano

    It sends every note over a USB cable (that's MIDI) to the Raspberry Pi.

  2. 02

    The Pi listens

    A Raspberry Pi running LEDsplay hears each note in real time and decides what to light.

  3. 03

    The keys light up

    An LED strip along the keyboard glows above the key you pressed, or the next key in a song.

  4. 04

    You choose on your phone

    Open ledsplay.local in any browser to pick songs, modes and colours.

FAQ

Short answers.

More detail in the install guide.

Which pianos work?

Any digital piano with USB MIDI output. LEDsplay is tested on a Roland FP-30.

Which Raspberry Pi do I need?

A Raspberry Pi Zero 2W or 4B. Both are tested. The Zero 2W fits the custom PCB and case; the 4B suits a standalone setup. A Pi 5 should also work but hasn't been tested yet.

Do I need to solder?

Not necessarily. If you'd rather not solder, buy an LED strip sold as 2 m in a single piece. The hardware list covers the details.

What does the trial include?

Every paid feature, for 14 days, with no card needed. After that, the free features keep working forever.

Is there a subscription?

No. The license is $9, paid once, for up to 3 devices. LEDsplay runs entirely on your Raspberry Pi, with no cloud and no account.
